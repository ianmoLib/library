**17/2/25**

Benefits of democracy:
1. Can get rid of an unsatisfactory government without violence.
2. Best protection of liberty — though can oppress minorities, especially with today's information tools.
3. Creates a political class with a wide range of opinions, mitigating against extremism.
4. More efficient — stops emergence of a small cabal operating without checks & balances.

Problem is fear and craving for security, along with a decline of political tolerance and the rise of moral absolutism.

Fundamental rights which must be protected by law:
- Freedom from coercion except by established legal authorities.
- Freedom from arbitrary detention, physical violence, injury or death.
- Recourse to an impartial, independent court.

Rights needed for a democracy:
- Freedom of thought and expression, assembly and association, in their broadest sense.
- Right to participate on equal terms with everyone else in fair and regular elections.
- Sufficient liberty to make use of these rights.

Clearly a democracy can legislate to curtail sexuality rights — so should courts be able to quash laws banning homosexuality? What about paedophilia if a parent/guardian consents and there's no coercion? Can I set up a whites-only men's club? Should any laws forbidding this be quashed?

**27/2/25** — David Graeber identified 3 freedoms:
1. Freedom to move away, relocate.
2. Freedom to disobey or ignore commands issued by others (rule of law?).
3. Freedom to shape entirely new social realities, or shift back and forth between different ones.

**2/3/25 — Re ECHR**

I have taken responsibility on myself to decide various political questions:
- How much privacy should we sacrifice in the interests of effective policing?
- How much liberty should be sacrificed to prevent the spread of contagious disease?
- How much freedom of speech should go in the interests of ethnic harmony or protection of personal reputation?

But it doesn't also add: how do we balance the right to family life against punishment for transgressions?

Do inalienable human rights exist outside of any difference between societies — be it political traditions, social conventions, moral and religious values, or physical conditions?
