**Ch.2 – Psychiatry and Neurology: Closing the Gap (6/1/25, 7/1/25, 8/1/25)**

Psychiatry & Neurology — closing the gap; mental as physical vs psychiatric vs neurological.

Nociceptive pain is a report via nervous system (CNS = Central) of something wrong; it's like a warning light on a car functioning correctly. Neuropathic pain caused by error (damage/disease) in sensory system that delivers pain info, or the processing of pain info, eg nerve damage. And there's been added nociplastic pain — when there's nothing wrong but the light is still on.

Precision is about estimation of reliability of data. If one is told to expect high level of pain, it hurts more — can be conscious (eg "this is going to hurt") or subliminal cues.

Wrong beliefs about pain intensity linked to pictures are reinforced each time they're experienced, rather than withering away. Placebos can affect deep structural pain as well as superficial. Precision weighting = Attention — if I'm looking for a needle in a haystack, I turn up the precision weighting of small silvery objects.

Hoover Test. Is it due to attention to a problem that's the issue — a functional problem rather than structural. Test looks to see if work of, say, the other leg gets done when patient is trying to do something where attention is elsewhere.

**Ch.3 – Action as Self-Fulfilling Prediction (9/1/25)**

Catching a ball: keep your eye on it, then move to counter changes in acceleration — linked via attention (precision weighting) and adjustments for errors in expectations; an ongoing dynamic process.

Driving fast in traffic — plan is to go as fast as possible while avoiding accidents or the police. Expert driver: constantly changing high-level prediction of how the car will behave, then lower-level sensory predictions acting as motor commands (gears, steering, brakes, head & eyes). Needs a highly trained inner model — experience. We don't learn the action, but how it will look & feel if we get it right.

Longer term goals — Realistic Optimism is order of the day. Mind, Body & World need to meet.

**Ch.4 – Predicting the Body (10/1/25, 11/1/25, 12/1/25)**

Why do we bother to do anything? The internal system needs to predict problems and act before they arise. Homeostasis = keeping the body within normal bounds; Allostasis = adapting to environmental changes (eg releasing cortisol to enable running away). Inward "interoceptive" system (hunger) vs outward "exteroceptive" (vision, threats) vs "proprioceptive" (motor control).

(Are we going to get to the question of where does self end and world begin — or are interoceptive & exteroceptive closely linked with fluid boundaries?)

Estimation of Error Dynamics — how well we're minimising prediction error vs expectation. Hitting all our shots = the Zone. The Goldilocks Zone — not too easy, not too hard. Learning is working on prediction errors — eg managing breathing to skip when skipping needs more oxygen than I can take in.

As humans, attracted to environments where a greater-than-expected number of prediction errors get resolved — a tendency towards play, learning, exploration. It's the absolute amount resolved, not the proportion — so novel environments give more prediction errors, more to go at, unless overwhelmed.

Emotions — an integration of predictions about bodily sensations and our understanding of the wider world; highly informed guesses. Too many and varied to be explained by changes in the body alone — need the wider context.

Emotions/feelings?? Thirst is a feeling — the result of a prediction that fluid will be needed soon, say in 20 minutes. Feeling goes away after drinking, as the prediction is now that the issue will be resolved in time.

Interoceptive predictive processing explains emotions — looking for high-level predictions of probable cause of changes in body info. The AIC (anterior insular cortex) combines interoceptive, proprioceptive, and exteroceptive info using its model of the world. This framing can change body sensations depending on whether there's a problem (?).

Is Depression caused by a failure of prediction-error signalling — you don't see the errors (resistance to new info is a symptom) and don't update the model. A random series of negative events can mess up the model leading to delusions, giving abnormally high precision to these & ignoring subsequent positive information.

Aesthetic Chills (They're Multiplying) — Salience detection hypothesis. Goosebumps/shivers when critical new information arrives — an "aha" moment, all falls into place; we can predict the future in a new, powerful way. Music does this by creating then resolving uncertainties. Attention (precision weighting) matters. Aesthetic chills are a physiological marker of a sudden increase in estimated importance (precision).

**Ch.7 – Hacking the Prediction Machine (18/1/25, 20/1/25, 24/1/25)**

Hacking the system: more intense means better placebos (surgery beats pills beats talking therapies); properly pressed white coats help. Honest placebos — maybe they "fool" the unconscious, or maybe it's just that someone's taking an interest? "Placebo" written on the box with better packaging works better.

You can train your body to produce a drug's effect — 3-4 real doses and the body "gets it," can do it with placebo alone. Some individuals become experts in "phenomenological control," exerting unconscious control over the shape of experience. Using VR to combat pain isn't just distraction — it subtly changes the predictions that build experience.

Remember: placebos can't fix a broken arm, and there's no evidence they replace anticoagulant or antiplatelet drugs.

Rituals and placebos impact the deep prediction engines that sculpt human existence, as well as talking remedies and comforting words — and we can use our own internal monologues the same way. Self-affirmation almost completely abolishes the gender gap in maths & spatial reasoning tests, and reduces the gap between deprived and affluent backgrounds.

Pain comes in 3 categories: Nociceptive (nervous system reporting actual tissue damage, eg a cut); Neuropathic (damage or disease in the nervous system); Nociplastic (abnormal processing of pain signals without tissue/nervous-system damage) — or a mixture.

Pain Reprocessing Theory — getting a patient to understand the pain isn't a warning that action is needed to stop further damage.

Hawkins, an IT worker, arranged his life using an algorithm with random variables to decide where he'd be and what he'd do. Psychedelics can have a similar "shaking the snow globe" effect — getting out of a rut and allowing new ways of seeing the world and "being you" to emerge.

Psychedelics: impact higher-level (more abstract) elements of the predictive model — who we are, what we want, how we perceive reality, rather than colour/shape/taste. Dampen prediction communication & the DMN; generally dampen brain activity. Leads to REBUS (relaxed beliefs under psychedelics) & reduced high-level predictions — therefore lower precision weighting at the top level, so we listen more to body & senses.

Meditation & control of attention — focused-attention meditation has a similar effect by upping precision on, eg, the breath, drowning out everything else.

**Conclusions – Ecologies of Prediction, Porous to the World (27/1/25)**

How can we utilise this understanding of a prediction model? Self-directed language, talking therapies (formal & informal), pain reprocessing strategies, meditation, psychedelics, packaging (stories), presentation & ritual.

We are what our predictive brain has built — a model of reality. Everything we experience is a construct of predictions plus sensory evidence. Prediction error signals result when current predictions fail to fit incoming evidence — the stronger our expectations (higher precision), the less impact counter-evidence has. Conscious mind is generally unaware of the predictions; our goals are highly predicted future states.

We train new minds via public language, writing systems, and cultural transmission, to use the collective wisdom of previous generations.

Our minds are a seething, swirling ocean of predictions continually orchestrated by brain, body & world. As we continue to build our world, we are building our minds.
