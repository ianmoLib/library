# The Matter with Things — Iain McGilchrist
*Notes: 27 Aug – 28 Dec 2023 (ongoing)*

A "Gestalt" is a whole that cannot be reduced to parts without loss of something essential to its nature - is that not every "object" (holon) which exists? If you take a Russian Doll apart it is only a potential Russian Doll; the essence of each being inside another is lost.

A percept is something noticed (perceived), and is different according to the attention we give it eg murder victim, or artist's model, or Aunt.... I guess the percept is just a holon.

Levels of Intelligence are decreasing at quite an alarming rate. Creativity is much more RH focussed - LH only joins in at the implementation stage, so INFP people must be more RH, but this is where they get their kicks, before implementation. He equates RH lesions (damage) with schizophrenia and autism - they manifest similarly, particularly inability to see wholes (holons) after breaking down into parts.

"We are controlled by our genes" is a LH schizophrenic type of response - breaking the object (the body) down into its parts. Perhaps we should view the body as a unique holon, not a platonic ideal to be broken down to see which bits aren't working correctly.

That which presences is represented by LH; so it is reality which presences. Jan Zwicky said "Truth is the result of attention (not inspection), of looking informed by love, of really looking." "Models" are just extended metaphors - eg the human body is like a machine, so you can mend it by replacing the bits, or by cleaning out the cancers (itself understood as a metaphor).

Assumptions made by science: the Universe is humanly comprehensible; comprehensible in purely physical terms; there is no purpose in nature; the principles that govern our part of the cosmos are fixed everywhere for all time; it depends on the idea of cause and effect; contraries are incompatible; only factual statements have validity; scientific enquiry can proceed objectively on the world "out there" by we "in here" (list not exhaustive). So now the book is on the machine model of the human body - the model doesn't work, so we need a new model, or can we ever escape from models? Fire is a process, unlike water - or can I view water as a process? A machine has a chain of actions; a living thing has a flow - how is there causation in a river?

McGilchrist argues reasoning is a post-hoc process to get to an already-held view, à la the Mercier & Sperber book I read previously. He's aware of that theory - you cannot exclude your predisposition when you make an argument. Is there ever a way to argue from first principles? It always depends on your first principles. Can I think of compelling arguments which change my point of view? More likely I'll change behaviour to achieve an inbuilt desire; desires change through experience, not argument. Philosophers use argument to fulfil their desires, he suggests.

Myth (Mythos) is rooted in body and emotion as well as cognition, whereas logos (logic, search for facts/truth) is very LH. Plato's ideal things (his solids) are the idea of facts outside experience. Music in humans is a means of group bonding, unlike birds, where it's competitive - to get the best mate.

For the horse whisperer, shutting off his left brain improved his results, but he also became more sensitive and more creative.

Subconscious can process lots more information than conscious - this is what it is to be an expert. *Ars longa, vita brevis.* Chess masters see patterns using RH. Nietzsche said the body is a great sage, a Many with One purpose, a war & a peace, a flock & a shepherd. Pigeons solve a 3-door problem straight away, whereas humans rationalise faultily and indulge in base rate probability matching (if it appears 67% of the time, choose it 67% of the time).

Intuition is the source of creativity - it comes in a flash. It's not the proper role of LH to check it for sense. Vitality is also an unconscious process, seemingly driven by desire. To what extent are physical processes autonomous - is the "decision" to burn fat made in the brain or in the organs? Our model/metaphor (are they the same thing?) is command & control - within that, what level of delegation is there? Writing something down organises thoughts and shows where they lead. Perhaps a role for consciousness is to focus on desire - "what do I want to happen" - short and long term, in alignment. So exercise is desired short-term rather than loafing, because it fulfils the dream of a long, happy life.

The world provides barriers to obtaining our desires so we push to achieve them. Trees need wind to become strong and survive long-term (Biosphere 2 project); Taleb's *Antifragile* referenced. We need suffering to grow.

"Groups cease to cohere if they have no criteria for exclusion." Book looks at sameness and uniqueness - uniqueness in each sameness, sameness in each uniqueness eg all wolves differ from each other, but none is "Sceal" - an Anglo-Saxon word for beach-dwelling vegetation - something like "shall," as in command ("we shall do such and such"), but also "is" or "does." "What I do is me: for that I came."

Jung distinguishes the Self (RH view of me) from Ego (LH view) - one is about the whole (conscious & unconscious), the other just conscious awareness. The Ego can be a good servant if transcended and included, but it's only a modality of action, not a seat of identity.

What is time, what is space - everything can be thought away except the mind; the mind can let go of anything except itself (Schopenhauer, 1819). Learning about Henri Bergson (early 20th century philosopher). An experience is like music, dependent on what comes before & after - there is flow, unlike a photo which is only an instant, and as Zeno demonstrated you can't build experience from instants. Perhaps we can modify the idea of holons to a continuous interplay of energy - matter & things are energy which we perceive as static and fixed.

Maybe Biodanza life is just change, in the sense of flow, rather than Descartes' series of static moments. To be human is to live, suspended between danger & opportunity (Lee Smolin).

Ruth Kastner: "I too have thought the RH corresponds to quantum-land possibilities & LH to spacetime actuals."

W. James's "Stream of Consciousness" - not a chain or train, because of its interdigital nature. It is a flow, like a river you cannot step in twice (or even once) - agrees with Bergson. Change when one sees "things" in a flow, but persistence when one sees the flow - things arise on the surface of flow - why do we prioritise "things"? There must be resistance to flow, which is where conceptual thought is - like eddies and whirlpools in the stream; things exist for a while then disappear.

"Vortexes"? Turbulent motion of fluids - turbulence is both source of resistance to flow and also the flow itself. Understandable to RH, alien to LH. Human consciousness sounds like a subset of... a distinct consciousness arises as a vortex within an endless flow, says Schelling, and that consciousness can come to know itself - it creates itself. "What it knows is the same as what is known."

At Creation, Weltseele (world soul) and World come into being together - they are one and the same process from 2 different standpoints.

Cerebellum, the "ancient brain," is much smaller than cerebrum but has 80% of our neurons, and more neurons means vastly more connections. Cerebellum controls motor activity, so surely it's acted on by music when new moves come forth in dancing. May be that stimulation of this area - which doesn't itself appear to have consciousness of its own (cerebrum, "new brain," is required for that, the book says) - new research shows the cerebellum involved in cognition and emotion as well as motor activity. The Cartesian model of duality of matter and thought continues to appear less viable, though it so much underpins language. Perception is founded on motion and cognition, not the other way round - it's an active process of going to meet the world, and it changes the world. Cognition itself is founded on motion.

Movement is everything - there cannot be stasis. Bergson said Intelligence (LH) starts ordinarily from immobility... Intuition (RH) from movement. "Everything flows" of Heraclitus is better than "everything changes" - it isn't things that flow but flow that creates things, eg eddies or mountains.

Japanese word *ma* means something like betweenness - about relationship, not discrete object (thing). Things emerge out of flow because of resistance, and flow is like a field. We need a combination of symmetry and asymmetry to generate something new.

Consciousness could mean "the experiential," so covers unconscious and preconscious, not just reflexive awareness (ie I am aware that I am aware).

Conscious thought may only be required when there's no habit to fall back on.
